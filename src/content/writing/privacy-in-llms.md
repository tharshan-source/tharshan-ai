You have the right to remain silent. Anything you say (to a chatbot) can and will be used against you in a court of law. You have the right to talk to a lawyer…blah blah blah…If you decide to answer with a chatbot instead…that’s on you.

Ofc your LLM convos can be used in court, just like any written communication. Not sure why this was breaking news this week. That panic says more about public misunderstanding than it does about the tech.

If you type something into a chatbot, it’s written evidence. If it’s timestamped, logged, or screenshotted, it can be subpoenaed. Treat it like a diary or co-conspirator? That’s how it’ll be treated in court.

And unlike a conversation with your lawyer, it’s not privileged. You don’t get confidentiality protections just because it feels private. OpenAI has been ordered by U.S. federal courts to retain user chats, even if you delete them. So much for ‘clearing your history.’ That data might (\*deffo) still exist.

This whole thing exposes **three current realities:**

1. **Digital hygiene is still bad** 🤮\
   No policy or encryption = it’s vulnerable. Simple.
2. **We confuse friendliness with trust** 🥴\
   It’s not a confidant. It’s a token predictor with no judgement.
3. **Hype is (still) blinding people (again)**🤫\
   AGI noise distracts from how these systems actually work today.

This isn’t an AI risk story. It’s a human one.

Your chatbot is not your lawyer. Not your friend. Not a conscious being (yet👀)\
And your inputs? Not invisible. It doesn’t matter if providers say your chats are “secure” with privacy always assume they’re not.

Today it’s “no training.”\
Tomorrow it’s a quiet TOS update or leaked checkpoint.\
And in a few years? They’ll pay the fine. You’ll hold the bag.

Because the incentives don’t lie. And neither does the training data.
