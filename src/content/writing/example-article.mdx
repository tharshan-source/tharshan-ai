The opening paragraph sits at full reading size. Write normally in Markdown: a blank line starts a new paragraph, `**bold**` for **bold**, `*italic*` for *italic*, and `[text](https://example.com)` for [a link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2022.103079).

## A section heading

Use `##` for main sections and `###` for sub-sections. They create anchor links automatically, so you can link to a section with `#a-section-heading`.

### A sub-section

Lists work as you'd expect:

- An unordered item
- Another item, which can run long enough to wrap onto a second line on a phone
- A third

1. A numbered item
2. A second numbered item

> A block quotation. Use it for quoting someone else's words, and attribute them in the surrounding text.

Footnotes are written like this.[^1] They collect automatically at the bottom of the article, with links back to where they were cited.[^2]

## Callouts

<Callout label="Note">
A callout is for an aside that the reader should not miss: a caveat, a definition, a method note.
</Callout>

<Callout label="My view" variant="view">
The `view` variant marks a passage as your own interpretation, which is useful in research notes to separate what the evidence says from what you think it means.
</Callout>

## Images

Put images in a folder next to your article (for example `src/content/writing/images/`) and reference them with standard Markdown: `![Alt text describing the image](./images/chart.png)`. They are resized and optimised automatically.

## Embeds

Embeds need an import line at the top of the file (see the three `import` lines under the frontmatter), which means the file must end in `.mdx`.

A YouTube video:

```mdx
<YouTube id="VIDEO_ID" title="Describe the video" />
```

A LinkedIn post:

<LinkedInEmbed urn="urn:li:activity:7358126103502168064" title="The WRAP Framework on LinkedIn" height={640} />

A TikTok: `<TikTok id="VIDEO_ID" title="…" />`, with `import TikTok from '../../components/TikTok.astro';` at the top.
